RESUME GUIDE

How to Improve Your Resume: Practical ATS & Resume Tips

A strong resume makes it easy for a recruiter to understand what you can do, what you have achieved, and why you are relevant to a particular job. Use the following checklist before submitting your next application.

1. Start with the target role

Your resume should make the type of role you want clear. Use a specific job title or professional summary rather than a vague introduction.

For example, instead of simply writing "Hardworking student", describe your actual area of interest and relevant skills, such as software development, data analysis, marketing, finance, or project management.

2. Tailor your resume to the job description

A single generic resume may not communicate your most relevant experience for every job. Read the job description carefully and identify the skills, responsibilities, tools, and qualifications that are most important.

Then make sure your genuinely relevant experience is easy to find in your resume. You can use the CareerMatch resume and job description matcher to compare your resume with a target role.

3. Use relevant keywords naturally

Recruiters and resume-parsing systems often look for terms related to the job. If a job genuinely requires Python, SQL, Excel, project management, or another skill that you actually possess, make sure your experience with it is clearly described.

Do not add skills you do not have simply because they appear in a job description. Your resume should accurately represent your experience.

4. Show achievements instead of only responsibilities

Whenever possible, describe what changed because of your work. Numbers can make achievements clearer when they are accurate.

For example, instead of "Managed social media", a stronger statement could be: "Planned and published 12 social media posts per month and increased engagement by 25%." Only use metrics that are truthful.

5. Keep your formatting simple

Use clear headings, readable fonts, consistent dates, and enough whitespace. Avoid excessive graphics, complicated columns, decorative text, or information that could make the document difficult to parse.

A visually attractive resume is useful only if recruiters can quickly find the information they need.

6. Put the most relevant information first

Recruiters may scan a resume quickly. Place your strongest and most relevant information where it is easy to notice.

For experienced candidates, this may include relevant professional experience. For students and freshers, relevant projects, internships, education, and demonstrated skills may be more important.

7. Make your skills easy to find

Include a concise skills section using terminology that accurately describes your abilities.

For technical roles, this might include programming languages, databases, frameworks, cloud platforms, and development tools. For other roles, it could include communication, research, financial analysis, project management, customer service, or other relevant capabilities.

8. Write strong project descriptions

If you are a student, fresher, or career changer, projects can demonstrate practical ability.

Explain what you built or accomplished, which technologies or methods you used, and what the result was. Avoid listing only the project title without explaining your contribution.

9. Check your resume before every application

Before submitting, check spelling, dates, contact information, formatting, job-specific keywords, and whether every important claim is accurate.

You can also compare your resume against the specific job description to identify skills and topics that deserve more attention.

10. Never claim skills you do not have

Resume optimization should improve how you communicate your real experience, not encourage you to misrepresent yourself.

If a job requires a skill you are currently learning, you can describe your learning honestly through a course, project, certification, or practice.

Resume checklist before applying

  • Is the target role clear?
  • Does the resume highlight relevant experience?
  • Are important skills easy to find?
  • Are achievements supported by accurate evidence?
  • Is the formatting simple and readable?
  • Have you checked spelling and dates?
  • Have you tailored the resume to the specific job?
  • Can you confidently explain every important claim?
