How to use CareerMatch

CareerMatch combines resume analysis, job-description matching, skill identification, and interview preparation into one simple workflow.

  1. Start by uploading your resume to the CareerMatch analyzer.
  2. Paste the job description for the position you are targeting.
  3. Review the recognised matching skills and potential gaps.
  4. Use the findings to improve your resume honestly and make relevant experience easier to find.
  5. Prepare for your interview using the interview question generator.

Why tailor your resume to a job?

Different jobs can prioritise different skills and experience. A resume that is suitable for one position may not clearly communicate the most relevant information for another.

Reading the job description carefully and tailoring your resume can help you present your relevant experience more clearly.

CareerMatch can help you compare your resume with the job description before you submit an application.

What does an ATS resume checker do?

Applicant tracking systems, commonly called ATS software, may be used by organisations to manage applications and process candidate information.

An ATS-oriented resume review can help you check whether your resume uses clear structure, relevant terminology, and information that is easy to identify.

The CareerMatch ATS resume checker provides a simple way to compare your resume with a target job description and review recognised skills.

Resume keywords: what should you include?

Job descriptions often contain terms describing required skills, technologies, qualifications, responsibilities, and experience.

If you genuinely have one of those skills, make sure your resume describes your experience with it clearly.

However, you should not add skills you do not possess simply because they appear in a job description. Resume optimisation should improve the communication of your real experience.

How to write stronger resume achievements

Instead of only listing responsibilities, explain what you accomplished. When accurate, numbers and measurable outcomes can make achievements easier to understand.

For example, rather than simply saying that you managed a project, explain what you delivered, what your contribution was, and what the result was.

For more practical advice, read the CareerMatch resume improvement guide.

Preparing for interviews

Good interview preparation is not about memorising perfect answers. Prepare genuine examples from your projects, studies, internships, employment, volunteering, or other relevant experiences.

For behavioural questions, the STAR method can provide a useful structure: Situation, Task, Action, and Result.

Use the CareerMatch interview question generator to practise questions related to your target role.

CareerMatch tools are designed for preparation

A resume match score or automated analysis should not be treated as a guarantee of an interview or job offer. Hiring decisions depend on many factors, including qualifications, experience, communication, interview performance, competition, and employer requirements.

Use CareerMatch as a preparation and review tool alongside your own judgement.
