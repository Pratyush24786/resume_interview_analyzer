Why practising interview questions helps

Interview preparation can make it easier to organise your experience and explain your decisions clearly. Practising does not mean memorising a script. Instead, prepare a few genuine examples that demonstrate your skills and experience.

Common interview question categories

Most interviews can include several types of questions, including questions about your background, technical knowledge, problem solving, teamwork, communication, motivation, and previous experience.

Use the STAR method for behavioural questions

The STAR method stands for Situation, Task, Action, and Result. It provides a simple structure for explaining what happened, what you were responsible for, what you did, and what happened as a result.

  • Situation: Explain the relevant context.
  • Task: Describe your responsibility or objective.
  • Action: Explain the actions you personally took.
  • Result: Describe the outcome and what you learned.

Prepare without memorising answers

Prepare two or three genuine stories from your projects, studies, internships, work, volunteering, or other relevant experiences. You can adapt those stories to different questions while keeping your answers truthful.

Prepare for the specific job

Interview preparation is more useful when it is connected to the role you are applying for. Review the job description, identify important skills, and prepare examples that demonstrate your experience with those skills.
