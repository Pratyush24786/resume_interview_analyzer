What is a resume keyword checker?

A resume keyword checker helps you identify important terms, skills, technologies, qualifications, and other relevant language that appears in a job description and compare it with the information in your resume.

CareerMatch can help you review recognised skills and relevant terms that appear in both your resume and the job description you provide.

This can make it easier to identify areas that may deserve further review before submitting an application.

Why are keywords important on a resume?

Job descriptions often contain terminology that describes the skills, experience, technologies, qualifications, and responsibilities associated with a role.

Using accurate terminology in your resume can make your relevant experience easier for recruiters and resume-processing software to identify.

However, keywords should always represent skills and experience you actually possess.

What are resume keyword gaps?

A keyword gap occurs when a relevant skill or term appears in a job description but is not recognised in your resume.

A potential gap does not automatically mean that you lack the skill. You may have the experience but describe it using different terminology, or the information may not be clearly represented in your resume.

Review each potential gap carefully before changing your resume.

Should you add missing keywords to your resume?

Only if they accurately describe your real experience.

For example, if a job description asks for Python and you have genuinely used Python in a project, internship, course, or professional role, you can make that experience clearer in your resume.

If you have never used a particular technology or skill, you should not add it simply because it appears in the job description.

Where should relevant keywords appear?

Skills section

Relevant technical skills can be listed in a dedicated Skills section when they genuinely represent your abilities.

Work experience

Where possible, demonstrate a skill through the work you actually performed rather than simply listing the skill.

Projects

Projects can be particularly useful for students, freshers, and career changers because they provide evidence of practical experience.

Certifications and education

Relevant courses, certifications, and academic work can provide additional context when they genuinely relate to the position.

How to use keywords naturally

Good resume writing is not about repeating the same keyword many times. Instead, use relevant terminology naturally when describing your genuine skills and experience.

For example, if you have used Python to build a data-analysis project, describe the project and your contribution rather than simply repeating the word "Python" throughout the resume.

Resume keywords vs keyword stuffing

Keyword stuffing means unnecessarily repeating terms in an attempt to increase a match result.

This can make a resume difficult to read and can misrepresent your actual experience.

A better approach is to use relevant terms naturally and support them with specific evidence of what you have done.

How to improve your resume after finding keyword gaps

  1. Review every potential missing skill.
  2. Decide whether you genuinely have that skill.
  3. If you do, make the relevant experience clearer in your resume.
  4. If you are learning the skill, represent your learning honestly.
  5. If you do not have the skill, do not claim it simply to improve a match.

Can keywords guarantee an ATS pass or interview?

No.

There is no universal ATS score or keyword threshold that guarantees that a resume will pass an employer's screening process.

Different organisations can use different recruitment systems and hiring processes.

Keywords are only one part of a well-prepared resume. Your qualifications, experience, achievements, readability, and overall suitability for the role also matter.
